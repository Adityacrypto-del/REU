=== SLIDE 5 ===
Literature Survey
PAGE 1 OF 2
1
Neural Collapse
   Liu & Qin, 2025
This work demonstrates that deep-layer feature representations collapse into tightly packed, class-wise clusters as training progresses. These structured geometric arrangements in the final layers indicate that the network has learned highly discriminative, compact representations. The phenomenon is known as Neural Collapse (NC) and provides strong evidence that CNNs develop structured internal representations, rather than arbitrary mappings. This is directly relevant to our study of layer-wise feature evolution.
2
Debiased Learning via Neural Collapse
   Wang et al., 2024
This paper investigates how the geometry of learned feature representations in deep networks directly influences learning dynamics and model fairness. The authors show that biased representations in intermediate layers contribute to model bias in predictions, and that explicitly controlling representation geometry during training can reduce this bias. This is relevant to understanding how feature structure across CNN layers links to classification behavior and fairness.

Load ResNet-18. Hook intermediate layers. Extract feature maps for each image.
Stage 2
Compute per-layer mean, variance, and sparsity. Plot activation statistics across depth.

Repeat Stages 1-2 on all 4 datasets. Compare patterns across datasets.

Apply PCA/t-SNE to feature vectors. Analyse neural collapse and cluster geometry.

=== SLIDE 13 ===
Literature Survey
PAGE 2 OF 2
3
Geometric Complexity
   Munn et al., 2024
This paper introduces geometric complexity as a quantitative measure to characterize how dataset structure affects the feature learning dynamics in neural networks. The authors show that datasets with higher geometric complexity lead to richer and more varied feature evolution across CNN layers. This provides a principled explanation for why different datasets produce different internal representations, which is a core question in our cross-dataset analysis framework.
4
Decision Boundary / Logits
   Hasegawa & Sato, 2024
This work examines the connection between internal feature representations learned by a CNN and the resulting decision boundaries formed in the logit space. The authors demonstrate that features at different layers shape how clearly classes are separated at classification time. Understanding this link is essential for our research, which aims to connect layer-wise feature statistics to model classification behavior and interpretability.
Background:  
General CNN Feature Hierarchy — CNNs learn hierarchical features from low-level edges to high-level semantic representations across layers.
